# PQD-001 Literature Tensions

## T1 — Revised versus Real-time Information

- Finding A: revised macro data often predicts bond or state-dependent returns.
- Finding B: real-time studies find that much predictability disappears, while newer nonlinear
  methods recover some predictive content.
- Conflict: information-set bias versus genuine nonlinear predictability.
- Unknown: whether revised regime labels also overstate conditional *factor* and volatility-managed
  performance, not just direct bond forecasts.
- Importance: empirical asset pricing should condition on information investors actually possessed.

## T2 — Volatility Management

- Finding A: inverse-volatility management improves several factor portfolios.
- Finding B: broad real-time tests find little systematic OOS improvement and unstable relations.
- Unknown: how much disagreement arises from revised state variables, leverage matching and estimator choice.

## T3 — Rebalance Timing

- Finding A: systematic strategies are often represented by one monthly or quarterly schedule.
- Finding B: practitioner research and newer ETF studies show material dispersion across calendar offsets.
- Unknown: whether conventional inference treats economically equivalent schedules as independent
  specifications or hides implementation uncertainty.

## T4 — Trend in Crises

- Finding A: trend is described as crisis alpha during sustained declines.
- Finding B: sharp rebounds generate momentum losses; recent work explains decay through microstructure.
- Unknown: whether ex-ante drawdown path measures add information beyond volatility and correlation.

## T5 — Anomaly Decay

- Finding A: post-publication returns decline materially.
- Finding B: international evidence and cost/arbitrage explanations differ.
- Unknown: whether decay classifications are robust to implementable price timing and common methodology.

## Working Title

**When Trend Components Break: Directional Predictability, Dynamic Risk Allocation, and Their Interaction**

## Fields

- Primary: empirical asset pricing
- Secondary: quantitative finance / portfolio choice

## Modified Primary Question

When the performance of a fixed time-series trend rule changes, do statistically identifiable
changes occur in conditional directional payoff, dynamic allocation value, or their interaction
after exposure, leverage, assets, timing and costs are matched?

The phrase “structural break” is not presumed. It must be supported by pre-specified or
unknown-break inference on publication-grade data.

## Contribution Boundary

Prior work separately questions directional predictability, attributes important performance to
volatility scaling, and documents instability in volatility-managed portfolios. It remains less
clear whether the directional and allocation components break together under one matched design.
The proposed study would test that joint component stability and quantify interaction without
interpreting arithmetic attribution as causality.

## Tier Assessment

- Tier A: currently unrealistic. It would require a clearer theoretical contribution, premier
  data, strong identification, extensive international evidence and expert econometric review.
- Tier B: possible only with broad institutional futures data, credible roll/cost construction,
  formal inference and a novelty check confirming no closer unpublished paper.
- Tier C: realistic with carefully documented public/index/ETF data, but conclusions must be
  narrower and cannot establish global managed-futures behavior.

## Stop Rule

No performance experiment proceeds until dataset access, exact estimands, primary tests and
break-date treatment are approved. Stop or pivot if the data cannot support at least Tier C
inference or if a closer paper already estimates the same joint decomposition.

# PQD-001 Finalists

## #1 — Macro Vintage Bias in Conditional Factor Evidence

- **Title:** What Did Investors Know? Macroeconomic Data Revisions and State-dependent Factor Returns
- **Field:** Empirical asset pricing / measurement
- **Question:** Do revised macro regime indicators overstate state dependence in factor and volatility-managed returns relative to real-time vintages?
- **Why it matters:** conditional asset-pricing conclusions should use the information set available at the time.
- **Tension:** revised-data predictability versus mixed real-time evidence.
- **Closest papers:** Ghysels et al. on rear-view bond forecasts; Wan et al. on real-time Bayesian bond prediction; Huang et al. on nonlinear real-time bond prediction.
- **What they establish:** vintage choice materially affects bond-return forecasting.
- **Not established:** whether vintage choice distorts conditional factor and scaling conclusions under a paired design.
- **Contribution:** measurement of hindsight bias in state-dependent factor evidence.
- **Mechanism:** macro revisions reclassify the investor information state.
- **H0:** real-time and latest-vintage interaction coefficients are equal.
- **H1:** latest-vintage estimates are systematically larger or otherwise different.
- **Estimand:** paired difference in pre-specified factor-state interaction coefficients.
- **Identification:** identical returns, dates and model; only vintage information changes.
- **Data:** ALFRED/FRED releases, French factor returns; optional university factor extensions.
- **Free version:** viable.
- **Sample:** longest common vintage window, defined by archive availability rather than results.
- **Method/inference:** paired panel/time-series interaction, HAC and block bootstrap; Holm correction.
- **Null meaning:** reassuring evidence that revision hindsight does not drive the literature result.
- **Failure mode:** regime construction still arbitrary or closest papers already extend to factors.
- **Feasibility:** high within 6–12 months.
- **Publication:** floor C; upside B with broad factors and strong positioning.

## #2 — Rebalance Timing Luck as Inference Uncertainty

- **Title:** One Strategy, Many Calendars: Rebalance Timing Luck and Inference in Tactical Allocation
- **Field:** Portfolio implementation / empirical methodology
- **Question:** Does treating all calendar offsets as one strategy family change conclusions drawn from a single rebalance schedule?
- **Why it matters:** economically equivalent investors can receive different outcomes from arbitrary calendar choice.
- **Tension:** widespread single-schedule reporting versus documented timing dispersion.
- **Closest papers:** Hoffstein et al. on rebalance timing luck; standard rebalancing studies; recent Chinese ETF schedule evidence.
- **Contribution:** family-level inference rather than another best-frequency comparison.
- **Mechanism:** discrete trading dates interact with clustered market jumps.
- **H0:** the published schedule conclusion is representative of the pre-enumerated offset family.
- **Estimand:** family distribution and single-schedule deviation.
- **Identification:** enumerate all offsets ex ante; no best-offset selection.
- **Data:** public ETF/index total returns.
- **Method:** randomization-style inference and block bootstrap.
- **Null meaning:** single-calendar convention is empirically harmless.
- **Failure mode:** contribution judged practitioner-only or mechanical.
- **Feasibility:** high.
- **Publication:** floor C; limited B upside.

## #3 — Drawdown Path Geometry and Trend Failure

- **Title:** Crisis Labels or Crisis Paths? Drawdown Duration, Rebound Speed, and Trend-following Outcomes
- **Field:** Momentum / crisis risk
- **Question:** Do ex-ante drawdown duration and rebound speed explain trend outcomes beyond volatility and correlation?
- **Why it matters:** sustained crises and sharp reversals imply different defensive properties.
- **Tension:** crisis-alpha evidence versus momentum crashes and rapid rebound losses.
- **Closest papers:** Daniel & Moskowitz; Hutchinson & O'Brien; Kurth et al.
- **Contribution:** externally defined path geometry rather than binary ex-post crisis labels.
- **Mechanism:** lagged signals require time to adjust and are exposed to rapid reversal.
- **H0:** path variables add no explanatory content.
- **Estimand:** incremental interaction between pre-defined path measures and next-period trend component returns.
- **Identification:** external event dates and definitions fixed before strategy outcomes.
- **Data:** public global indexes for Tier C; futures upgrade for broader claims.
- **Method:** event panel/local projections with clustered or block-bootstrap inference.
- **Null meaning:** path narratives do not improve on volatility/correlation controls.
- **Failure mode:** path variables remain endogenous and independent event count is small.
- **Feasibility:** moderate.
- **Publication:** floor C; upside B only with broad futures/events.

# Referee #2 — Recommendation: Reject

The manuscript recombines established findings rather than supplying a new economic result.
Kim et al. already separate unscaled direction from volatility scaling and document subperiod decay;
Huang et al. undermine directional predictability; Goyal and Jegadeesh show why exposure matching
changes inference; Cederburg et al. formally document instability in volatility management. Recent
working papers are still more damaging: Sepp and Lucic derive exact trend P&L identities, while
Kurth et al. document and explain post-2009 trend decay in roughly 100 futures.

Calling the residual term “interaction” does not create identification. In
`Σ(w-b)s r`, allocation and signal are mechanically multiplied, so H2 and H3 are not separately
identified without an independently specified allocation counterfactual or factorial intervention.
The decomposition is an accounting identity, not evidence about an economic mechanism.

Structural-break analysis is especially vulnerable. The motivating split came from contaminated
pilot evidence, the number of independent asset-class clusters is tiny, and common shocks invalidate
naive panel precision. An unknown-break test can date instability but cannot explain it. State
interactions would be equally unconvincing if states are selected after inspecting R5.

The proposed mechanisms are a menu, not a theory. Continuation, positive unconditional means,
volatility timing, correlation, reversal, crisis convexity and costs predict overlapping patterns.
Without a discriminating restriction, the paper cannot tell which explanation is responsible.

Data feasibility is also fatal in the current setting. Nine ETFs over roughly sixteen years cannot
support credible multi-asset structural inference. Free continuous-futures sources lack consistent
roll, multiplier and historical cost metadata; vendor data may be unavailable to the researcher.

At best, the paper would put known components into one table. That is insufficient for publication
beyond a student replication exercise. I recommend rejection unless the author identifies a new,
testable economic implication not contained in the closest papers and obtains a broad contract-level
futures dataset.

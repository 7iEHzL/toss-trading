# Adversarial Novelty Audit

## Result

- Closest-paper threat: `HIGH`
- Most dangerous paper: Kurth, Eisler, Rej & Bouchaud (2026), *Is Trend Still Your Friend?*
- Mathematical threat: Sepp & Lucic (2026), *The Science and Practice of Trend-Following Systems*
- Novelty after red team: `WEAK`
- Joint analysis creates new information: `PARTIAL AT BEST`
- Economic mechanism: `UNRESOLVED`

## Contribution Necessity Test

Estimating H1/H2/H3 jointly is useful only if it tests a restriction that cannot be recovered by
juxtaposing prior papers—for example, whether the signal coefficient and allocation contrast share
a common break conditional on an independently measured mechanism. Merely reporting all three in
one table is not a contribution. No defensible new restriction or independently grounded mechanism
has yet been established.

## Genuine Remaining Possibility

A narrower measurement paper could construct a factorial matched-risk decomposition and show how
naive attribution changes when leverage, volatility and common shocks are controlled. That is a
method/measurement contribution, not yet an economic contribution, and is likely Tier C unless a
new mechanism or broader evidence emerges.

## Not New

- Direction versus volatility scaling
- Trend performance decay and subperiod instability
- Sell-signal reversal and rebound losses
- Correlation-dependent allocation
- Option-like crisis behavior
- Exact or mechanical trend P&L decomposition

## Decision

Candidate 1 fails the GO rule because novelty is below MODERATE after red-team review, the economic
mechanism is unresolved, H2/H3 are only conditionally separable, and publication-grade data access
is unconfirmed. Decision: `MODIFY`, with no empirical execution authorization.

# Five-minute Advisor Pitch

I am studying why trend-following results appear unstable, not trying to optimize a trading rule.
The central question is whether changes in performance come from the directional signal, dynamic
risk allocation, or their interaction after implementation is matched.

The literature already makes this difficult. Kim, Tse and Wald show that volatility scaling explains
much reported TSMOM alpha. Huang and coauthors question directional predictability. Cederburg and
coauthors show volatility-managed relations can be unstable. Two recent working papers go further:
one derives exact trend P&L decompositions, and another documents post-2009 futures trend decay and
proposes a microstructure mechanism. So merely putting signal and scaling in one table is not new.

The possible remaining contribution is narrower. I would define four portfolios that cross fixed or
dynamic directional exposure with fixed or dynamic allocation, while matching leverage, volatility,
assets, timing and costs. Their difference-in-differences gives a descriptive interaction. I would
then test a single externally motivated continuous state variable rather than choose a break from the
data. This is not causal unless stronger identifying variation exists.

The project can fail in three ways: a closer paper may already answer the question; the interaction
may be only mechanical; or I may not obtain a sufficiently broad contract-level futures dataset.
Nine ETFs are not enough for the intended claim.

I need advice on whether the narrowed interaction/stability question is a real finance contribution,
which mechanism could be specified before seeing results, and whether the university provides
Bloomberg, Datastream or another reproducible futures database. Until those questions are answered,
I will not run performance tests or use the sealed final sample.

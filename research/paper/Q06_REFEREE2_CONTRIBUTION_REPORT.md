# Referee #2 Contribution Report — Q06

## Recommendation

**Reject the current project before empirical design.**

## Hostile report assuming large, significant results

The manuscript repeats a known information-set critique. Ghysels, Horan and Moench derive the generic
predictive-regression problem and demonstrate it in Treasuries. Pierdzioch, Döpke and Hartmann already
compare real-time and revised macro data for stock-return forecasting. Favero, Melone and Tamoni already
condition FF5 returns on macro trends and present real-time-output results, while Vincenz and Zeissler
use vintage-aware macro predictors for a broad factor allocation. Replacing their return object or adding
a paired column is not an independent contribution.

The paper offers no economic mechanism predicting which factor families should be most affected.
Business-cycle exposure is not exposure to subsequent agency measurement error. He, Su and Yu cannot
fill the gap: their treatment is subjective expectation updating and their mechanism operates through
contemporaneous optimism, financing and investment, not future official revisions.

The design also fixes a modern factor file even though Akey, Robertson and Simutin show factor histories
change across vintages. That choice may isolate MacroVintage for a modern-researcher estimand, but it
invalidates any language about historical investability. Searching across macro series, factor families,
regimes or transformations would immediately create a multiple-testing and factor-zoo problem. Results
would have weak external validity because both macro revisions and factor reconstructions are source- and
period-specific. Similar real-time-data findings in bonds, aggregate equities, currencies and factor timing
make a large coefficient gap unsurprising.

## Strongest honest author response

The paper would not claim investability or a new return-forecasting method. It would use a paired,
vintage-only comparison to quantify one reproducibility threat for conditional factor research. Fixing
FactorVintage is deliberate experimental control; Akey et al. define a separate sensitivity dimension.
The null is informative because aggregate-equity evidence shows revision effects need not transfer, and
the factor setting is important in contemporary empirical asset pricing.

## Does the response win?

**No.** It defends internal validity and usefulness as a careful replication/measurement note, but it does
not supply the missing independent economic prediction. The referee's “obvious extension” objection
still wins for a publication-oriented finance paper.

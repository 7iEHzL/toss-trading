# PQD-001 Research Territory Map

| Territory | Established | Tension / unresolved issue | Data reality |
|---|---|---|---|
| Momentum/trend | Past-return continuation and crash/reversal evidence | Direction, scaling and recent decay mechanisms overlap | Global futures usually institutional |
| Volatility management | Scaling can improve some factor portfolios | Broad real-time evidence is weak and unstable | French factors and volatility indexes are public |
| Asset allocation | Diversification and rebalancing are standard | Calendar choice can create timing luck and overstated inference | ETF/index pilot is public |
| Portfolio implementation | Cost, leverage and timing materially alter returns | Many papers report one arbitrary rebalance schedule | Public prices; historical costs weaker |
| Anomaly robustness | Many anomalies fail standardized replication | Data construction, costs and publication decay explanations conflict | French/open factor libraries useful |
| Cross-sectional pricing | Hundreds of predictors proposed | Multiple testing and microcap dependence remain central | Security-level work usually needs CRSP |
| Return predictability | Macro/yield predictors often appear significant | Revised versus real-time data can reverse conclusions | ALFRED/FRED is public |
| Regime instability | Conditional effects are frequently reported | Regime labels are endogenous and revised ex post | Real-time vintages offer a measurement design |

The strongest undergraduate opportunity is a measurement question using public real-time vintages,
not another optimized strategy. The most crowded territory is generic trend decay/decomposition.

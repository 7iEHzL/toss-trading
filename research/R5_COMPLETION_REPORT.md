# R5 Completion Report

## Status

`R5 COMPLETE — STRONG TEMPORAL/COMPONENT INSTABILITY; NO TRADING CANDIDATE`

R5 tested one frozen long/cash multi-asset trend plus inverse-volatility rule. It performed well
in 2007–2014 and poorly in the already-observed 2015–2022 period. R5-003 attributes the
difference primarily to weaker directional payoff and a reversal in allocation value, with costs
amplifying rather than causing failure.

## Final Interpretation

`EVIDENCE OF STRONG TEMPORAL/REGIME DEPENDENCE`

- Stable trend premium: not supported by this sample.
- Primarily risk allocation: not sufficient; weighting contribution also changes sign.
- Primarily directional predictability: the larger descriptive source, especially missed rebounds
  after negative signals, but not a proven causal explanation.
- Crisis concentration: early success was not concentrated in the NBER GFC recession window;
  the short COVID recession window was negative.
- Publication novelty: temporal instability, reversal and volatility-scaling effects are already
  substantially documented. R5 alone is not a novel paper result.

## Research Disposition

- Do not adopt R5 as a trading strategy.
- Do not rescue or tune R5.
- Preserve R5 as a pilot dataset and motivating empirical fact.
- Move from strategy search to publication-question selection.
- Keep Final OOS 2023–2025 SEALED.

## Provisional Next Question

Does structural instability in time-series momentum originate in directional conditional payoff,
risk allocation, or their interaction when assets, leverage, timing and costs are held constant?

This requires broader and longer data plus formal inference; the current nine-ETF sample cannot
support a publication-level answer.
